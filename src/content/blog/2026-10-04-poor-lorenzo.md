When I get distracted, I try to disrupt myself to pull out of the loop:

- Stuck on a hard problem at work? No doomscrolling or screwing around on the internet. Instead, I often go to [TypingTest.com](https://www.typingtest.com). Take a 2 minute typing test, see if I broke my record, and then get back to the original task. Feels slightly productive, and it's time-boxed.
- Or maybe I need to change locations, but there's a lot of "setup cost" with doing that.

Working with people can be frustrating. When that happens, I try to disrupt myself...maybe it's passive-aggressive? But, I get nice. Overly nice, or weirdly creative. It's hard to describe, but here's an example.

In 2020, I took a job to work locally and be in the office. The day I started was the first day of COVID lockdown. Cool. Now I was just one of five people in the US working for a company with hundreds of engineers and project managers overseas. They were all on site together. It was COVID and I was in my house.

We were an engineering shop with split duties. The US team handled client relationships and project requirements; the overseas team executed. My overseas partner was our project manager. He would join the client calls. He then rallied the engineering team and helped get the work delivered. With eleven time zones between us, you could keep a non-stop delivery pace. Unless you got behind.

My days started at 7am and ended around 11pm to keep pace and keep things moving.

To make it a little better, I would write a handoff note in Slack at the end of every day. Stats, priorities, where things stood, where they needed to be by morning.

He rarely read them. Even more rarely responded. To top it off, we would sync up on calls first thing in the morning to check in on progress before client meetings. Time after time, the update he gave me would be contradicted a few moments later in front of the client. I would give the update, "I'm sorry, but the date of our milestone may need to be pushed." Then my "partner" would unmute: "That's not true, Anthony. We'll get that out for you by the end of the day and beat the original deadline."

WTF, man.

That lit a small, hot fire in me.

When I get that frustrated, I tend to go one of three ways. I get overly nice. I get *obnoxiously* nice. Or I let weird, frustrated creativity be the outlet.

This time, I decided to write the story of a horse race.

My daily updates on Slack now included a short saga of emojis and creative writing. I needed to get this punk to read the updates and give some kind of acknowledgement. Every one of those sat right under the day's real work. Ticket numbers, client asks, blockers. The race was the dessert you only got if you scrolled past your vegetables.

And then, one night, a reply.

![Slack reply at 3:27 AM: "Poor Lorenzo 😞"](/images/2026-10-04-poor-lorenzo/01-poor-lorenzo.png)

3:27 AM. Months of handoff notes, and the thing that finally got a response was a cartoon jockey lying in elephant dung.

I laughed. Sat with it for a while. Then started my new job a few months later.

Poor Lorenzo. He never did stay on that horse.
