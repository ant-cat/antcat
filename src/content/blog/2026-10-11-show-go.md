Music is so hard to discover; the only way to find it is by giving it time and a chance. And by chance is how I've discovered some of my favorite bands. They're either an opening act, or someone at work shared them after stumbling across them live, or the algorithm actually finds me and pushes their KEXP/Tiny Desk my way.

That problem of discovery is what led to Show Go, and I’ve been carrying it around for about 11 years. Back in 2015/2016 we had a hackathon at Ignite and made nearly 0 progress. Instead, it ended up being a small concept deck. No code or anything, just some concepts stuck in a PowerPoint. Dev/APIs used to be very hard. It would take days to get an API key. 2026, a POC was built in 24hrs.

Ok, let's get into it:

Jacksonville is a smaller touring market. We get some larger arena shows, and we get some tiny bar shows. But there’s only a handful of medium-sized venues that are technically in other cities: Ponte Vedra and St. Augustine. It's a big chance to take on unknown bands, especially with ticket prices etc. A band name, an album cover, even a genre label tells you almost nothing about what a band sounds like. That’s even more true for the smaller acts.

And as parents, we only get a handful of nights out.

## What it does

Back in 2009, I wrote here about wanting to see shows at [Cafe Eleven](/writing/2009-09-22-two-shows-i-really-want-to-see-join-me) in St. Augustine, and about a band called BLK JKS that I had never heard of until I watched a video on the venue’s website. Same itch. I just never had a way to scratch it for every show in town.


Show Go turns every live show near you into a preview radio station.

You pick a place, a radius and a date range (by default, last week through four months out). Show Go gathers every show in range and plays 30 seconds of every band on every bill, openers included, one after another.

While it plays, you can:

- **Keep** a band, which saves their dates and ticket links into a list for planning a night out
- Say **Not for me** and never hear them again
- Hear **another song** from the same band
- **Watch live** to see what they’re like on stage
- Flip the running order to **Openers first**, because those are the bands nobody has heard of yet

[Try it out →](https://show.ant.cat/)

It’s a test right now. The POC was to test if the concept could even work and be interesting to listen to. Honestly, my first time turning it on I found two brand new bands immediately that are now on heavy rotation: Florist and Pinegrove. Never heard of them, they are older indie bands. But boom! It worked.


There are a lot of features to add to make this good such as genre and venue filtering. Expect rough edges, but I could not be more excited!

## The stack

- **Next.js 14** and **TypeScript**, built on the same patterns as [Baby is a Go](/writing/2026-09-22-baby-is-a-go)
- **PostgreSQL** on **Neon**, with **Prisma**
- **Ticketmaster** and **SeatGeek** for show listings
- **iTunes** for the 30-second previews (Deezer is wired in as a backup, though it hasn’t found a single one yet)
- **OpenStreetMap** to turn a city name into a spot on the map
- **Deployed on Vercel** at show.ant.cat

Claude and I built it over a few evenings. The first night produced a single-file prototype I could open on my laptop. Then we rebuilt it as a real app meant for a few dozen people spread across different cities. Searches are cached by area, so if ten people near Austin tune in, the listing sites only get asked once.



This is where we're at.

## What I actually learned

**The data is the product.**

I thought the hard part would be the radio. It wasn’t. The hard part is that two ticketing sites can describe the same night at the same room and agree on very little. Ticketmaster says “Metro,” SeatGeek says “Metro Chicago.” One says “Bar-Be-Que,” the other says “BBQ.” The map pins for a single venue were up to 16 miles apart.

When I added SeatGeek, the number of shows went up, and so did the duplicates: 21 in Austin, 29 in Chicago. The fix came from looking at the actual rows instead of guessing. The city on a listing turned out to be more trustworthy than its map pin. A couple of rounds later, Austin was down to zero duplicates and Chicago to two, and those two are a real disagreement between the sources about which casino a show is at.

**The thing I care about most is the hardest thing to find.**

Openers are the whole point of Show Go, and they’re mostly missing. About 70% of the shows in my test cities list only the headliner. Listings exist to sell tickets, and headliners sell tickets. The opener is an afterthought in the data, the same way it’s an afterthought on the marquee.

That’s the next problem to solve, and it’s a good one to have, because it’s the exact gap the app exists for.

**“No answer” is not the same as “no.”**

The first night I tried it on my phone, it played a few bands and then crossed off everything after them. It looked like nobody in Denver had any music.

The real problem:

iTunes allows about 20 preview searches a minute from one connection. The app was looking ahead and skipping fast past bands without previews, so it burned through that in about a minute. Every search after that failed, and the app read each failure as “this band has no music.” Worse, it saved that verdict to the shared database, so every other listener would have lost those bands too.

It was fixed in two ways:

Searches now run one at a time with a short gap between them. And only a real “no match” counts as no music; if iTunes doesn’t answer, the band gets tried again instead of crossed off. The backup service I’d wired in, Deezer, turned out to have found zero previews in every test, so iTunes is doing all the work.

## What’s next

- Openers: Bandsintown and JamBase both track full lineups
- Filtering out the things that aren’t bands (festival names, comedy nights, a gospel brunch)
- Design changes, now that I can judge them on the live site
- A real test in the car: it plays on my phone now, but I haven’t tried a whole drive with the screen locked

The name might change too. I fell for “Hear Here” until I found out an audio app already owns it. Show Go it is, for now.
