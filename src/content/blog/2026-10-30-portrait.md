I have a Spotify Car Thing sitting on the left side of my monitor. Spotify gave up on it at the end of 2024, but [Bridgething](https://bridgething.com) brought it back to life with new software, a launcher, and room for whatever apps you want to throw on it.

Sitting there horizontal though…it just looked odd. A wide little slab off to the side of my desk, waiting for a car that was never coming.

Stand it up and it clicks. It takes up less room, it stays at eye level, and it stops feeling like a car accessory. It feels like a small phone.

One problem: the Car Thing has no idea it's standing up.

## What it does

Portrait is a Bridgething app that turns the Car Thing into a vertical now playing screen.

![Portrait's now playing and lyrics screens, standing upright](/images/2026-10-30-portrait/01-now-playing-and-lyrics.webp)
*Now playing on the left, synced lyrics on the right (demo data, the band is made up)*

- **Big art, big buttons.** Album art up top, then the song, a progress bar you can drag, and play, skip, shuffle, repeat and like.
- **Lyrics that scroll with the song.** Didn't expect this to be my favorite part, but lyrics just look right tall. Tap any line and the song jumps there.
- **The dial still works.** Turn it for volume (or scrub, or skip, your pick). Press it to play or pause.
- **Hold back to flip it.** Dial at the top or dial at the bottom, whichever way you stood it up.
- **Its own volume bar.** The built-in one pops up sideways, so Portrait draws a tall one.

It plays whatever Bridgething is connected to. From my phone that's Spotify or Apple Music. From the desktop app it's anything playing on my computer, even YouTube in a browser.

## The trick

The screen is 800 by 480, landscape, forever. Bridgething can't rotate it.

So Portrait doesn't ask. It builds a tall 480 by 800 page and turns the whole thing 90 degrees before it draws. The Car Thing thinks it's showing a normal wide app. I see a phone.

![What the Car Thing's screen actually draws: the app lying on its side](/images/2026-10-30-portrait/02-what-the-screen-draws.webp)
*What the screen actually draws. Turn your head.*

I figured touch would be the hard part: tap the screen, hit the wrong button. Nope. The browser handles the math through the turn, so a tap lands right where your finger is. We tested it by tapping a quarter of the way along the sideways progress bar on a 3:34 song, and it jumped to 0:53. Boom!

## The icon

It needed a logo. I wanted it to feel vertical and easy, and to say the thing I love most about it: it plays whatever I've got going on my desktop. Spotify, YouTube in a browser, whatever.

So it's the Car Thing standing up, dial up in the corner, a big play button and a little bit of sound.

![The Portrait app icon: a tall gradient Car Thing with a play button and sound bars, shown large and at launcher size](/images/2026-10-30-portrait/03-portrait-icon.webp)
*The Car Thing standing up, playing whatever's on.*

## What I actually learned

**Good docs make hardware feel like software.** Bridgething ships a starter project with React, a dev server, and a script that pushes straight to the device over USB. Claude read through the docs and the source, and the first working version came together in a single evening. Most of the time went into the small stuff, like which way the dial should turn and where the volume bar should sit.

**The constraint was the idea.** "The screen can't rotate" sounds like a dead end. It turned out to be the whole design: build it tall, turn it, done.

**The little things that bug you are worth fixing.** This was never a big problem. It just bugged the heck out of me every time I looked left. Those are my favorite things to fix.
